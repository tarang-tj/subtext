# Demo video script

**Hard limit 3:00.** Judges dock a point per ten seconds over. This script lands at about 2:45 read at a normal pace, which leaves room to breathe.

Record the screen at 1280x720 or larger, browser zoomed to about 125% so text is legible on a laptop. Use the app's own Large text setting; it is a feature and it looks like one on camera.

Do not show a face. Do not use music. Both cost you audio clarity, which is the thing that actually gets scored.

---

## 0:00 – 0:22 — The problem, with the message on screen

> **On screen:** the Decode tab, the message `can we talk later` already pasted in.

"This is a real message. If you are autistic, this message costs you the rest of your afternoon.

Not because you cannot understand subtext. That is the assumption most tools in this space are built on, and the research says it is wrong. Autistic adults read indirect requests about as accurately as anyone else when they have the context.

The problem is that text throws the context away. Tone, timing, what happened this morning, all gone. The person who sent this was leaning on context they never actually gave you."

---

## 0:22 – 1:05 — Decode

> **Action:** click Decode this message. Let the result appear. Do not talk over the loading.

"So Subtext does not guess.

It tells you what the text actually settles, which here is almost nothing. It gives you the readings that are genuinely live, with honest numbers, not one confident answer dressed up as insight.

It names what the sender left out, and it says the part that matters: this is missing from the message, not from you.

And then it gives you something you could actually send."

> **Action:** scroll to the clarifying question. Leave it on screen for a beat.

"No apology. No hedging. Just the question."

---

## 1:05 – 2:05 — Forecast and the refusal

> **Action:** switch to the second tab. Load the group-project example: *You didn't send the dates. Send them today so I can finish my part.*

"The other direction is where most tools in this category quietly do harm.

Here is a message an autistic teenager might send. It is clear. It is not rude. But a non-autistic reader will often decide it is."

> **Action:** click Forecast. Let the misreadings render.

"Subtext tells you how it may be misread, and it puts that where it belongs. Non-autistic observers misjudge autistic people and then rate them badly based on their own misreading. That is the reader's error, so it is described as the reader's error.

And then it tells you to send it as written."

> **Action:** scroll to the masking block, or to the blocked example. Hold on it.

"Here is the part I want to show you properly. The obvious feature here is a button that makes the message softer. We did not build it. We built the thing that stops it.

Camouflaging is associated with anxiety, depression, and thwarted belonging. So when the model tries to turn this into 'sorry, would you maybe be able to send those when you get a chance', the server catches it and blocks it, and shows you exactly which words it tried to add.

Adding context is self-advocacy. Adding hedges is masking. That line is enforced in code, not in a prompt, because a language model will promise not to hedge and then hedge anyway."

---

## 2:05 – 2:30 — Receipts and the honest bit

> **Action:** open the "Where this comes from" panel.

"Every screen shows the research behind it, marked where the source is autistic-authored, with what each paper actually changed in the build.

And every screen has this."

> **Action:** point at "This does not match my experience".

"Because I have to be straight with you. This was built from published research in one week. It has not been tested with an autistic teenager yet. I am not going to turn a literature review into a user study and call it co-design.

So the tool says where it came from, it says that research is not lived experience, and if they disagree it tells you the research is the thing that needs updating."

---

## 2:30 – 2:45 — Close

> **On screen:** back to the top of the page.

"The research changed this project twice. It killed the original concept, and it deleted the one feature everybody expects.

That is what designing with the evidence instead of around it actually looks like. The testing protocol is in the repo, ready to run.

Subtext. Text strips context. This puts some of it back, both ways."
